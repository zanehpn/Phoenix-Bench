The bug is in `src/dmi_jtag_tap.sv` around the `trst_no` generation logic.

### What’s wrong
`trst_no` is being driven from a small reset-sync style process that updates on `tck_i` and `trst_ni`, but:
- `trst_sync1` is assigned combinationally from `trst_ni`
- `trst_sync2` is registered
- `trst_no` is updated inside the same `always_ff`

This creates a glitch-prone, poorly defined reset pulse generation path. Since `trst_no` is then used as an asynchronous reset in `dmi_jtag.sv` and `dmi_cdc.sv`, any transient on `trst_no` can asynchronously reset logic in a dangerous way.

### Fix strategy
Make `trst_no` a clean, direct, synchronous representation of the TAP reset state, and avoid generating a glitchy intermediate reset signal. The safest minimal change is to:
- remove the broken sync-chain logic
- drive `trst_no` directly from the actual JTAG reset behavior in a controlled combinational assignment derived from the TAP FSM state

A robust and simple interpretation is:
- `trst_no = 0` while TAP is in `TestLogicReset`
- `trst_no = 1` otherwise

That keeps reset asserted during the JTAG Test-Logic-Reset state without introducing extra asynchronous pulse-generation logic.
